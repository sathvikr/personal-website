Love as identity

February 2026

Consider the following definition of love (following Aphorism 153): “How much A loves B is how much of A's identity B comprises.” If B is a greater portion of A's identity than A themselves, then A loves B more than A loves themselves. Clearly, this formulation of love ascends morality: human beings take actions which optimize reward within their own moral frameworks; because B is more of A's identity than A themselves, A assigns greater reward to taking actions optimizing B's happiness and quality of life; and thus, A invents new values for B, inherently leaving old, now-untenable Biblical values behind as dust; and thus, good and evil are re-valued in accordance with B's flourishing. Christians can now rationally explain loss of faith and general intelligence by simply claiming God loves us in this way: Mankind is a nontrivial part of God's vast—infinite—identity; God takes Good not as that which follows the tree of righteousness, but as that which INCREASES flourishing of mankind; God takes Evil not as that which opposes the tree of righteousness, but as that which DECREASES flourishing of mankind.
