title: "Relativity (Escher)"
date: "2026-06-18"
---

Aim: Design a perspective-based game where:

1. The player initially doesn't know the rules and must figure them out.
2. If the player knows the rules, the game is trivial.
3. Level $n$ introduces a counterexample to what the player thinks the goal is.

The game is *perspective-based* because it is possible for the player to possess a sub-par world model (perspective) (i.e., one that only perfectly holds from Level 1 to $i$) and still beat the game. World models are built in varying abstraction hierarchies, as shown in [\[1\]](#fn-relativity-1):

<figure class="note-image-wide">
<img src="img/novelgames.png" alt="Two-panel diagram: (a) an abstraction hierarchy of world models, from observations at Level 1 up through a structured probabilistic instance model to increasingly abstract principles at Level n; (b) a parallel game hierarchy, from broad game category down through sub-category, instance, and level to interactions within a level.">
</figure>

This is analogous to Relativity (e.g., the staircase in one orientation is a roof in another).

<p class="note-footnote" id="fn-relativity-2">Loose thoughts: if a platformer, the game map might be similar to Relativity. What seems like a block in 2D could be a door in 3D; progressing across levels in 2D in a scrolling platformer is in reality walking through doors in a 3D choose your own adventure; the 3D choose your own adventure could have interesting topologies, like a Mobius strip taking you back to the start, but with different mechanics only visible in 3D (so if the player still has a 2D world model, they have to build a fake <em>mod 2</em> rule that doesn't capture the real universe's mechanics).</p>
