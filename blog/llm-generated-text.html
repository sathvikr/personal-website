First, much of the blog post is forming arguments based off current LLM capability (e.g., “have you seen LLM word choice?”) which I think is unwise, especially for a future audience. Second, while I am not of the position that writing should be done with LLMs, I am of the position that LLMs also have interesting things to say (and if not yet, eventually).

  • No mental element behind the thoughts. But the purpose of writing is to reveal a mental element. Disagree, the purpose of writing can be to invoke a mental element in the reader. LLM writing can achieve this.
  • Why LLM it up? Just give me the prompt. Why share graphics? Just give me the Python that produces it. In fact, don’t give me a game; give me the source code and a compiler. Maybe it is not a single pass but iterative development.
  • If the LLM said something surprising and you did investigate it, you can clearly write something better. This is not attacking the moral premise of publishing LLM speak, and thus does not hold when AI becomes a better writer than any human. That will be pretty soon.

How much you enjoy a poem is some function of the quality of the text and how much you can put yourself in the author’s shoes. With LLMs, most people only get the first component. That doesn’t mean the poem is unlikeable, but it does mean there is less inherent potential than a human-written text.
